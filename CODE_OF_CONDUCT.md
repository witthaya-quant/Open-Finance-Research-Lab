# OFRL Code of Conduct

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Effective date:** 2026-11-04  
**Applies to:** OFRL repositories, Issues, Pull Requests, Discussions where enabled, project spaces, publications, and events operated under the OFRL name

## 1. Purpose

OFRL brings together people who may have different disciplines, experience, methods, professional backgrounds, and opinions. A productive research environment depends on rigorous criticism and mutual respect.

This Code of Conduct defines the minimum standards for participation. It protects the ability to question a method or conclusion without turning technical disagreement into personal hostility.

## 2. Expected behaviour

Participants are expected to:

- Treat others with respect, professionalism, and good faith.
- Critique research questions, assumptions, calculations, implementations, evidence, and conclusions—not a person's identity, intelligence, credentials, or worth.
- Explain disagreement with specific reasoning and evidence where possible.
- Acknowledge uncertainty, correct errors, and give accurate attribution.
- Make room for different levels of experience and different technical approaches.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, intellectual-property rights, and data restrictions.
- Follow the repository's research, contribution, licensing, and security policies.

## 3. Unacceptable behaviour

The following are not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, stalking, or personal attacks.
- Humiliation or exclusion based on credentials, seniority, employment, education, nationality, sex, disability, age, or other personal characteristics.
- Retaliation against someone for raising a concern, reporting misconduct, submitting a good-faith review, or disagreeing with a decision.
- Deliberate misrepresentation of another person's work, statements, or research findings.
- Publishing another person's private information, confidential material, credentials, or restricted data without authority.
- Repeated disruption, bad-faith review behaviour, spam, or attempts to use personal pressure to bypass research or governance requirements.
- Plagiarism, false attribution, or knowingly presenting another person's work as one's own.

Strong technical criticism, rejection of a proposal, and evidence-based disagreement are not misconduct by themselves.

## 4. Scope

This Code applies to conduct in OFRL-controlled spaces and to conduct outside those spaces when it is directly connected to OFRL participation and materially affects the safety or integrity of the community.

Project maintainers may set additional conduct expectations for specific events or collaboration environments, provided they do not weaken this Code.

## 5. Reporting a concern

Do not use a public Issue or Pull Request to report harassment, threats, doxxing, private information, or other sensitive conduct concerns.

During the founder-maintained stage, report concerns privately to the Founder / Principal Maintainer through the contact channel published on the repository owner's GitHub profile: https://github.com/witthaya-quant

When reporting, include the relevant URL or evidence, a factual description, approximate dates, and any immediate safety concern. Share only information necessary to assess the report.

At the Organization stage, OFRL should appoint at least one additional confidential reporting contact who is not the subject of the report. The contact route must be published here before the Organization opens participation broadly.

## 6. Review and enforcement

During Stage 0, the Founder / Principal Maintainer is responsible for receiving and assessing reports. If the Founder is the subject of a report, the reporter should use GitHub's platform-level reporting mechanisms and OFRL should not claim to provide an independent internal appeal where no independent officer exists.

At later stages, reports should be handled by a designated Maintainer or conduct contact who has no material conflict in the matter. A person named in a report must not decide the outcome of that report.

Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and may include:

1. A private request to stop or correct the behaviour.
2. A warning and documented expectations.
3. Temporary restrictions on participation or repository access.
4. Removal from a specific project or Maintainer role.
5. Removal from OFRL spaces or termination of access for serious or repeated misconduct.
6. Referral to GitHub or appropriate authorities when required.

Before a material sanction, the person concerned should normally have an opportunity to respond, unless immediate action is reasonably necessary to protect people, data, or systems. Decisions should be based on available evidence, handled discreetly, and documented with access limited to those who need to know.

OFRL will not promise absolute confidentiality where disclosure is required to assess a report, protect someone, comply with law, or respond to a platform investigation.

## 7. Enforcement principles

Enforcement should be consistent, proportionate, and free from retaliation. The status, seniority, technical reputation, or contribution volume of a person does not exempt them from this Code.

A good-faith report that cannot be substantiated is not itself misconduct. Knowingly fabricated reports or deliberate retaliation may be addressed under this Code.

## 8. Relationship to other policies

## 9. Review

The Founder / Principal Maintainer owns this Code during Stage 0. Material amendments require a Pull Request and a recorded governance decision. Review it before opening broad community participation and whenever enforcement responsibilities change.
