## 1. Problem and motivation

What problem or question do you want to investigate? Why does it matter in financial, business, mathematical, statistical, or engineering practice?

## 2. Research question and intended use

State the primary question. Explain who may use the result and what decision, analysis, or implementation it could support.

## 3. Background and common foundations

Identify relevant established concepts, equations, methods, standards, or prior research. Define specialist terminology where it is necessary for reviewers to understand the proposal.

## 4. Proposed method

Explain the core idea, key equations or logic, assumptions, inputs, outputs, and intended operating conditions.

## 5. Alternatives and comparison

Identify established or alternative methods worth examining. Explain whether the proposal aims to develop one method, reproduce an existing method, compare approaches, or combine methods.

Do not assume that novelty means superiority. The proposal may conclude that an established method remains more appropriate.
