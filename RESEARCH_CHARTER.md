# OFRL Research Charter

## 1. Purpose

Open Finance Research Lab (OFRL) exists to develop and share rigorous, reproducible research across financial modelling, quantitative finance, investment analysis, financial engineering, and computational financial analytics.

The lab is designed around a practical premise: useful financial research should connect sound reasoning with a transparent implementation and evidence that can be independently examined.

OFRL's broader foundation, vision, mission, and principles are set out in [FOUNDATION_AND_DIRECTION.md](FOUNDATION_AND_DIRECTION.md). This Charter translates that direction into the lab's research scope, operating standards, and accountability.

## 2. Vision

To build an open, evolving body of financial knowledge, research methods, and practical tools that people can inspect, test, use, adapt, and improve together.

## 3. Mission

To bring together people with different experiences and areas of expertise to investigate financial and business problems, develop and examine alternative methods, and turn useful research into reproducible models, software, and practical resources.

OFRL does not seek to enforce one universal methodology. It provides a disciplined environment in which different approaches can be explained, tested, compared in context, and improved. Openness to different methods does not mean that every method or claim is equally supported; conclusions remain accountable to evidence.

## 4. Scope

OFRL may investigate the following disciplines:

1. Quantitative Finance Research
2. Financial Modelling & Valuation
3. Equity Research & Investment Analysis
4. Fixed Income & Credit Research
5. Derivatives & Volatility Research
6. Market Microstructure & Liquidity
7. Portfolio & Risk Analytics
8. Corporate Finance & Capital Markets
9. Financial Data Science & Machine Learning
10. Research Methodology & Model Validation

The tenth area is cross-cutting: its standards apply to projects in all other areas.

## 5. Research model

OFRL follows an Industry-to-Research process:

- **Industry observation:** identify recurring competencies and analytical problems from legitimate public sources.
- **Competency mapping:** describe the underlying knowledge and methods without copying protected job-advertisement text.
- **Problem formulation:** define a research question, hypothesis where appropriate, and measurable criteria.
- **Method exploration:** identify relevant established approaches and reasonable alternatives where the research question calls for comparison.
- **Independent development:** design and implement the method independently.
- **Validation:** test mathematical correctness, implementation integrity, data suitability, sensitivity, robustness, and failure modes.
- **Comparison:** interpret results against stated objectives, assumptions, constraints, and suitable alternatives; do not turn methodological differences into judgements about contributors.
- **Publication:** release a clear account of the method and evidence, subject to data rights and disclosure boundaries.

Industry relevance is a source of research questions, not a substitute for independent reasoning. Comparison is meaningful only when the context and evaluation criteria are sufficiently clear.

## 6. Standards of evidence

Every material claim should be proportionate to the evidence supporting it. Projects must distinguish among:

- Mathematical or accounting correctness.
- Correctness of a software implementation.
- Statistical evidence in a specified dataset and experimental design.
- Robustness across relevant assumptions, samples, or regimes.
- Economic relevance after costs, constraints, and implementation considerations.
- Independent replication.

Passing one category does not imply passing the others. In particular, predictive accuracy does not by itself establish an exploitable trading opportunity.

## 7. Research integrity

OFRL expects contributors to:

- State assumptions, data sources, transformations, and exclusions.
- Identify uncertainty and limitations.
- Avoid look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, leakage, and selective reporting where relevant.
- Report negative and inconclusive findings when they are methodologically sound.
- Correct material errors openly and preserve an understandable change history.
- Distinguish original work from external sources and third-party contributions.
- Critique methods, assumptions, implementations, and claims rather than attacking contributors.

## 8. Data and intellectual property

OFRL will use data only where the relevant access and use terms permit the intended activity. Each project must document provenance, relevant restrictions, and whether redistribution is permitted.

The lab will not publish confidential employer information, client information, private research, or proprietary trading strategies. Public code or documentation must not be treated as permission to redistribute third-party datasets, vendor content, or other protected materials.

## 9. Governance and accountability

OFRL is founder-led at its current stage. The founder is responsible for research direction, project selection, publication standards, and disclosure decisions. Contributors retain clear attribution for their own work.

Founder-led governance does not make the founder's opinions or methods immune from challenge. Contributors may propose alternatives, question assumptions, provide independent implementations, and replicate published findings. Decisions about work published under the OFRL name should be based on project relevance, reasoning, evidence, quality, rights, and maintainability—not on personal status or agreement with the founder.

Review comments and independent replication should be recorded where practical. A project may document multiple approaches when they serve different contexts or when evidence does not support choosing one over another.

OFRL's governance applies from the founder-maintained stage. [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) defines the current single-maintainer controls, future contributor and Maintainer roles, decision rights, review requirements, and the conditions for transferring the repository to a GitHub Organization. Any change of stage or authority must be explicit and documented.

## 10. What OFRL is not

OFRL is not:

- A provider of investment recommendations or personalised financial advice.
- A signal-selling, copy-trading, or automated execution service.
- A guarantee of investment performance or trading profitability.
- A substitute for regulated financial, legal, accounting, or tax advice.
- A repository for confidential or proprietary materials.
- A competition to rank contributors by credentials, seniority, or personal status.
- A forum in which every proposed idea is accepted without examination.

## 11. Amendment

This Charter may evolve as the research programme and contributor community develop. Material changes should be documented in version history so the lab's standards remain inspectable. Changes should remain consistent with the foundation and direction document unless an intentional change in direction is explicitly explained and recorded.
