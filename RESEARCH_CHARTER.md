# OFRL Research Charter

## 1. Purpose

Open Finance Research Lab (OFRL) exists to develop and share rigorous, reproducible research across financial modelling, quantitative finance, investment analysis, financial engineering, and computational financial analytics.

The lab is designed around a practical premise: useful financial research should connect sound reasoning with a transparent implementation and evidence that can be independently examined.

## 2. Mission

To build an open, collaborative body of finance research that makes methods understandable, models testable, and findings reproducible.

## 3. Scope

OFRL may investigate the following disciplines:

1. Quantitative Finance Research
2. Financial Modelling & Valuation
3. Equity Research & Investment Analysis
4. Fixed Income & Credit Research
5. Derivatives & Volatility Research
6. Market Microstructure & Liquidity
7. Portfolio & Risk Analytics
8. Corporate Finance & Capital Markets
9. Financial Data Science & Machine Learning
10. Research Methodology & Model Validation

The tenth area is cross-cutting: its standards apply to projects in all other areas.

## 4. Research model

OFRL follows an Industry-to-Research process:

- **Industry observation:** identify recurring competencies and analytical problems from legitimate public sources.
- **Competency mapping:** describe the underlying knowledge and methods without copying protected job-advertisement text.
- **Problem formulation:** define a research question, hypothesis where appropriate, and measurable criteria.
- **Independent development:** design and implement the method independently.
- **Validation:** test mathematical correctness, implementation integrity, data suitability, sensitivity, robustness, and failure modes.
- **Publication:** release a clear account of the method and evidence, subject to data rights and disclosure boundaries.

Industry relevance is a source of research questions, not a substitute for independent reasoning.

## 5. Standards of evidence

Every material claim should be proportionate to the evidence supporting it. Projects must distinguish among:

- Mathematical or accounting correctness.
- Correctness of a software implementation.
- Statistical evidence in a specified dataset and experimental design.
- Robustness across relevant assumptions, samples, or regimes.
- Economic relevance after costs, constraints, and implementation considerations.
- Independent replication.

Passing one category does not imply passing the others. In particular, predictive accuracy does not by itself establish an exploitable trading opportunity.

## 6. Research integrity

OFRL expects contributors to:

- State assumptions, data sources, transformations, and exclusions.
- Identify uncertainty and limitations.
- Avoid look-ahead bias, survivorship bias, leakage, and selective reporting where relevant.
- Report negative and inconclusive findings when they are methodologically sound.
- Correct material errors openly and preserve an understandable change history.
- Distinguish original work from external sources and third-party contributions.

## 7. Data and intellectual property

OFRL will use data only where the relevant access and use terms permit the intended activity. Each project must document provenance, relevant restrictions, and whether redistribution is permitted.

The lab will not publish confidential employer information, client information, private research, or proprietary trading strategies. Public code or documentation must not be treated as permission to redistribute third-party datasets, vendor content, or other protected materials.

## 8. Governance and accountability

OFRL is founder-led. The founder is responsible for research direction, project selection, publication standards, and disclosure decisions. Contributors retain clear attribution for their own work. Review comments and independent replication should be recorded where practical.

As the lab grows, governance processes may be expanded through documented contributor roles, review procedures, and project maintainers. Any such change should be explicit rather than implied.

## 9. What OFRL is not

OFRL is not:

- A provider of investment recommendations or personalised financial advice.
- A signal-selling, copy-trading, or automated execution service.
- A guarantee of investment performance or trading profitability.
- A substitute for regulated financial, legal, accounting, or tax advice.
- A repository for confidential or proprietary materials.

## 10. Amendment

This charter may evolve as the research programme and contributor community develop. Material changes should be documented in version history so the lab's standards remain inspectable.
